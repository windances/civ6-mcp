# City captures, T228-T299: four assaults, one failure, and what each cost

The window between 布鲁塞尔 and the Dutch campaign holds four attempts at a city: two taken well
inside their windows, one taken slowly, and one that expired with the army still walking. It is the
first stretch of this match where assaulting is the *standing* work rather than an exception, so it is
also the first place the doctrine's own numbers can be checked against it.

Sources: the five task files (`done/016`-`done/022`, `tmp/023`), the diary
(`.civ6-mcp-data/diary_china_-1894041591.jsonl`), and the MCP logs
(`.civ6-mcp-data/log_china_-1894041591_*.jsonl`, filtered to `turn >= 228`). Every count below is from
those logs unless it names a task file.

The three-phase procedure was executed every time: a probe attack produced the wall numbers where the
city tile was fogged (T235), `get_staging_plan` was run for every target (T234, T254, T260, T283, T285 -
five calls, no assault without one), and the fire was concentrated enough to finish 布鲁塞尔 in three
turns of shooting. `tactics/07`'s gate reached the right verdict twice: 021's target was the one the
map said was out of reach, and the file that inherited the lesson (023) opened by re-counting its
deadline from real coordinates.

One in three of those stops is a unit that was ordered to a staging tile and stopped short because
another of our units was in the corridor: T234 ordered eight units and took eight `STOPPED_MID_PATH`,
one to three tiles short. The plan assigns distinct tiles but says nothing about the **order of the
calls**, so a column ordered nearest-first queues behind itself. Every staging plan in the window left
6-9 units unplaced (T254: 9 of 10), which is the same fact seen from the other end.

Two units were **disbanded** to stop the bleed (023's `overrides:` records the moment). The cause is on

expedition and the home file takes what is left**. The rule was right and the spend went ahead anyway -
`carrying-capacity` was red on five of the turns above.

## The checks that stayed red while the army was fighting

`use-your-attacks` failed on most siege turns (T235-T241, T246-T247, T252-T253, T256, T261-T262,
T265-T270, T286-T295); `cut-the-supply` was red almost continuously, and 哈勒姆's supply line was cut
**3/6** while the city healed about twenty a turn; `siege-train` (2/3) and `ranged-mass` (2/4) were red
from T274 and T267 respectively and are still red at T294 - the two Artillery upgrades that clear the
first one cost 155 gold each *with* the discount card. `mass-on-contact`, `screen-the-siege` (T292) and
`idle-district-slot` filled in the rest.

The detection worked. What did not happen is the **response**: a red check was read, recorded and then
carried into the next turn. That is the largest single gap this window exposes, because every one of
those rules was measuring a real cost that the log can now price.

## Still open

- **The stop count is told, not enforced.** The tool now names the order and the test pins it, but
  nothing stops a session from issuing them the other way. The measurable target for the next window is
  `STOPPED_MID_PATH` per staging turn: 3.2 over T228-T299 (19 on the worst turn).
- **`cut-the-supply` accepts a partial cut.** 3/6 was enough to be reported, not enough to stop the
  heal. Whether the rule should require a complete ring, or whether a partial cut slows the heal enough
  to be worth the units, has not been measured - it needs the city's own healing line across a few turns
  with a partial cut.
- **A red check has no consequence.** The TURN START briefing quotes failures and the plan answers them
  in prose; the two establishment checks (`siege-train`, `ranged-mass`) have been red for twenty turns
  while a 155-gold upgrade each would clear them. The upgrade rules cover the classes; what is missing
  is anything that makes a *long*-red check cost something.

# Military production experiments - the cross-attempt report

The experiment is `docs/experiments/README.md`, and this file is its **result**: what each attempt
measured, which doctrine claim survived it, which was corrected, and **which artifact each correction
became**. An entry that lands nowhere is a diary note, so every correction below names its sink on the
ladder (code + test, a check rule, a task file, the directive, a tactic, or this directory).

Attempts: **A1** (`001-attempt-A1.md`, `A1-T40.json`) and **A2** (`002-attempt-A2.md`, `A2-T40.json`, and
the attempt's own end snapshot `A2-final.json` - **A2 ended at T68 with the city kept**).

## 0. How to review this, in three commands

Everything below is computed from the raw record (`diary_<game>.jsonl` and `log_<game>_<session>.jsonl`),
so a reader can re-derive rather than trust it:

```
# 1. the two attempts on identical columns - establishment, army start, siege order,
#    first keep, science and gold per turn, the H5 count, self-report mismatches, red rules
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\experiment-report.py --compare docs\experiments\A1-T40.json docs\experiments\A2-final.json

# 2. the attempt's own four questions, answered from the record (Q2 is the ordering after Engineering)
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\experiment-report.py --game china_911679432 --run sacred-garnet-vault-35,pale-pearl-aqueduct-92,volcanic-indigo-caravan-23 --from 1 --to 68 --verdict --questions a2

# 3. the whole suite, including the instrument's own tests (1156 passed, 3 skipped at the time of writing)
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pytest tests/ -q
```

**Two things a re-run must be told, or it will mislead.** `--run` takes **every session** the attempt
spanned (A2 is three: T1-T40, T41-T65, T66-T68) - the instrument attributes diary rows by session time,
and naming one session drops the others. And the snapshots keep the **measuring definitions they were
taken with**: `ESTABLISHMENT` was corrected after A2 (the ram left the required table, recon and
anti-cavalry entered it), so replaying these logs with today's instrument is not comparable to the saved
reports - the two snapshots are, because they were both taken under the same one.

## 4. What the experiment has cost and what it has bought

- **A1:** 274 tool calls over 40 turns = **6.8 a turn**, against the ~39 a turn the previous match
  measured at T272-T287; the ten-turn reviews answered with numbers each time; five self-reports, all
  agreeing with the record.
- **A2:** 268 calls over 40 turns = **6.7 a turn** in the first half; 11 self-reports, **11 of them
  wrong** on the same column; one attack order discarded on a reply that could not be read as a result.
- **The second half costs 2.7 times as much per turn, and the army is why.** T41-T59 is **345 calls over
  19 turns = 18.2 a turn**, of which `unit_action` alone is **177 calls (9.3 a turn)** - one order per
  unit per turn, plus retries, for 13 units. The first half ran the same doctrine at 6.7 a turn with 11
  units and almost no fighting; A1 ran 6.8 with 9. So the orchestration cost tracks the *army on the
  board and in contact*, not the empire: building the train is cheap to run, marching and fighting it is
  not. That is the number to weigh against the previous match's ~39 a turn at T272-T287, which was a war.
- **A2's whole cost curve, session by session** - the same empire, the same doctrine, and the phases the
  cost belongs to: T1-T40 **268 calls (6.7/turn)**, building; T41-T65 **384 (15.4/turn)**, marching,
  cutting supply and taking the first shots; T66-T68 **83 (27.7/turn)**, the assault and the capture.
  **A2 is 735 calls in total** against A1's 274, and the last three turns alone are 11% of it. A campaign
  is cheap to plan and expensive to *run*, and the curve is the number a future attempt should budget
  with rather than the average.
- **What A1 bought:** three corrections to the experiment itself (the scenario, the hypothesis, the
  stop rule), one instrument defect, and a map-geometry rule that the doctrine could not have known -
  all of them about *how the question is asked*, which is the only thing a first attempt can honestly
  settle when the map has already answered it.
- **What A2 bought:** four more corrections, and they are of a different kind - the **window** (H1 is
  unaskable inside 40 turns whatever the map says), the **recon slot** the doctrine never names, the
  instrument's **role map** (blind to the Heavy Chariot, found by the executor disagreeing with it),
  and the **combat reply** that reads like a failure when it is a hit. Two of the four were found

  defect visible at all.
